Introduction to Cybersecurity

Beginner 30 minutes 5 lessons

What is Cybersecurity?

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting computers, servers, mobile devices, electronic systems, networks, and data from digital attacks. These attacks are usually aimed at accessing, changing, or destroying sensitive information, extorting money from users, or interrupting normal business processes.

Why is Cybersecurity Important?
  • Protect Personal Information: Your personal data like photos, messages, and financial information
  • Prevent Financial Loss: Cybercriminals can steal money from your bank accounts or mobile money
  • Maintain Privacy: Keep your private conversations and activities secure
  • Business Protection: Companies need to protect customer data and business operations
  • National Security: Countries need to protect critical infrastructure and government systems
Common Cyber Threats in East Africa
Mobile Money Fraud

Criminals trick people into sending money through fake SMS messages or phone calls pretending to be from mobile money services like M-Pesa, Airtel Money, or MTN Mobile Money.

Phishing Emails

Fake emails that look like they come from banks, government offices, or trusted companies, asking you to click links or provide personal information.

Unsecure Wi-Fi

Using public Wi-Fi networks without protection can allow criminals to see your internet activity and steal your information.

Malicious Apps

Fake apps that steal your personal information, send premium SMS messages, or access your contacts and photos without permission.

Basic Protection Principles
Strong Passwords

Use different, complex passwords for each account

Regular Updates

Keep your devices and apps updated with latest security patches

Stay Alert

Be suspicious of unexpected messages, emails, or phone calls

Backup Data

Regularly backup important files and information

Password Security

Beginner 25 minutes

Creating Strong Passwords

Passwords are your first line of defense against cybercriminals. A strong password can prevent unauthorized access to your accounts, while a weak password makes you an easy target.

What Makes a Password Strong?
At least 12 characters long - Longer passwords are harder to crack
Mix of uppercase and lowercase letters - Like "MyPassword123"
Include numbers - Add digits throughout the password
Use special characters - Like !, @, #, $, %, &, *
Avoid personal information - Don't use your name, birthday, or phone number
Password Creation Methods
Method 1: Passphrase Technique

Create a memorable sentence and use the first letter of each word, adding numbers and symbols.

Example:
Sentence: "I love eating ugali with sukuma wiki every Sunday at 7pm!"
Password: "IleuWsweSa7p!"
Method 2: Word Combination

Combine 3-4 unrelated words with numbers and symbols.

Example:
Words: "Elephant", "Coffee", "Mountain", "Blue"
Password: "Elephant#Coffee9Mountain$Blue"
Method 3: Substitution Method

Replace letters with numbers and symbols that look similar.

Example:
Original: "NairobiKenya2024"
Substituted: "N@1r0b1K3ny@2024!"
Common Password Mistakes to Avoid
Using the same password everywhere - If one account is hacked, all accounts are at risk
Using personal information - Names, birthdays, phone numbers are easy to guess
Common passwords - "password123", "123456", "qwerty" are easily cracked
Writing passwords down - Don't write passwords on paper or save in notes
Sharing passwords - Never share your passwords with anyone
Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)

Two-factor authentication adds an extra layer of security by requiring two forms of identification:

  1. Something you know - Your password
  2. Something you have - Your phone (for SMS codes) or an authenticator app
Pro Tip

Enable 2FA on all important accounts like email, banking, and social media. Even if someone steals your password, they still can't access your account without your phone.

Internet Safety for Kids

Kids 20 minutes

Welcome to Internet Safety! 🌟

Hi there! The internet is like a big playground with lots of fun things to do, but just like any playground, we need to know how to stay safe while having fun!

🔒 What is Internet Safety?

Internet safety means knowing how to protect yourself when you're online. Just like you look both ways before crossing the street, you need to be careful online too!

👥 Stranger Danger Online
🚨 Important Rule: Never talk to strangers online!
  • Don't accept friend requests from people you don't know
  • Never share your real name, address, or school with strangers
  • If someone asks to meet you in person, tell an adult immediately
  • Don't send photos of yourself to people you don't know
🔐 Keeping Your Information Secret
Information to NEVER share online:
Your home address
Your phone number
Your school name
Your passwords
Your birthday
Your full name
🎮 Safe Gaming Online
Voice Chat: Only use voice chat with friends you know in real life
Gaming Friends: Don't add strangers as gaming friends
Time Limits: Take breaks and don't play for too long
Report Bullies: Tell an adult if someone is mean to you online
📱 Social Media Safety
Rules for Social Media:
  1. Only accept friend requests from people you know in real life
  2. Keep your profile private (only friends can see your posts)
  3. Think before you post - would you be okay if your parents or teachers saw it?
  4. Don't post photos that show where you are right now
  5. Be kind to others - don't say mean things online
🆘 What to Do If Something Goes Wrong
1
Don't Panic! Take a deep breath
2
Tell a Trusted Adult immediately (parent, teacher, guardian)
3
Don't Delete Anything - adults might need to see what happened
4
Block and Report anyone who makes you uncomfortable
🌟 Fun Fact!

The internet connects over 4 billion people around the world! That's more than half of all people on Earth. By staying safe online, you can explore, learn, and have fun with people from everywhere!

Mobile Money Safety

Important 35 minutes

Protecting Your Mobile Money

Mobile money services like M-Pesa, Airtel Money, MTN Mobile Money, and Tigo Pesa have revolutionized financial services in East Africa. However, with convenience comes the need for security awareness.

Common Mobile Money Scams
Scam 1: Fake Reversal Requests

How it works: Scammer sends you money, then calls claiming it was sent by mistake and asks you to reverse it.

The trick: The original transaction will be reversed by the mobile money company, but your reversal to the scammer won't be.

Protection: Never reverse transactions based on phone calls. Tell callers to contact their mobile money provider directly.
Scam 2: Fake Customer Service

How it works: Scammer calls pretending to be from M-Pesa, Airtel Money, etc., asking for your PIN or personal details.

The trick: Real customer service will NEVER ask for your PIN over the phone.

Protection: Hang up immediately. Call the official customer service number yourself if you need help.
Scam 3: Fake Promotions

How it works: SMS claiming you've won money, asking you to send a small amount to "claim your prize."

The trick: There is no prize. You lose the money you send.

Protection: Legitimate promotions never ask you to pay to claim prizes. Verify with the official company.
Scam 4: SIM Swap Fraud

How it works: Scammer gets a new SIM card with your phone number, then accesses your mobile money account.

The trick: They use social engineering to convince your mobile network provider to transfer your number.

Protection: Set up additional security with your mobile provider. Monitor your account regularly.
Mobile Money Security Best Practices
PIN Security
  • Use a unique PIN that's different from your phone unlock code
  • Don't use obvious numbers like 1234, 0000, or your birth year
  • Change your PIN regularly (every 3-6 months)
  • Never share your PIN with anyone, including family
  • Cover your phone when entering your PIN in public
Phone Security
  • Always lock your phone with a PIN, pattern, or biometric
  • Don't save your mobile money PIN in your phone
  • Log out of mobile money apps when not in use
  • Don't let others use your phone for mobile money transactions
  • Report lost or stolen phones immediately
Transaction Monitoring
  • Check your balance regularly
  • Read all SMS confirmations carefully
  • Keep records of important transactions
  • Report suspicious transactions immediately
  • Set up balance alerts if available
Safe Transaction Practices
  • Double-check recipient phone numbers before sending money
  • Only transact with people you know and trust
  • For business transactions, verify the recipient's identity
  • Be cautious with large transactions
  • Use official mobile money agents only
What to Do If You're Scammed
1
Act Quickly: Contact your mobile money provider immediately
2
Change Your PIN: Change your mobile money PIN immediately
3
Report to Police: File a report with local police
4
Keep Evidence: Save all SMS messages and call logs
5
Warn Others: Tell friends and family about the scam

Recognizing Phishing Attacks

Critical 40 minutes

Understanding Phishing Attacks

Phishing is like digital fishing - criminals cast a wide net hoping to catch unsuspecting victims. They use fake emails, SMS messages, websites, and phone calls to steal your personal information, passwords, or money.

Types of Phishing Attacks
Email Phishing

Fake emails that appear to come from legitimate organizations like banks, government offices, or popular websites.

Example: Email claiming to be from your bank saying your account will be closed unless you click a link and enter your login details.
SMS Phishing (Smishing)

Fraudulent text messages designed to trick you into revealing personal information or clicking malicious links.

Example: SMS saying "Your M-Pesa account has been suspended. Click here to reactivate: [malicious link]"
Voice Phishing (Vishing)

Phone calls where scammers impersonate legitimate organizations to extract sensitive information.

Example: Caller claiming to be from your bank asking you to verify your account by providing your PIN and personal details.
Website Phishing

Fake websites that look identical to legitimate sites, designed to steal your login credentials.

Example: A fake banking website with a URL like "kcb-bank.com" instead of the real "kcbgroup.com"
Red Flags to Watch For
Email/SMS Red Flags
  • Urgent language: "Act now!" "Your account will be closed!" "Limited time!"
  • Generic greetings: "Dear Customer" instead of your actual name
  • Spelling/grammar errors: Professional organizations proofread their communications
  • Suspicious sender: Email from "bank@gmail.com" instead of official domain
  • Unexpected attachments: Files you weren't expecting
  • Requests for sensitive info: Asking for passwords, PINs, or personal details
Website Red Flags
  • Wrong URL: Slight misspellings of legitimate websites
  • No HTTPS: Missing padlock icon in browser address bar
  • Poor design: Low-quality graphics or layout
  • Pop-up warnings: Fake security alerts
  • Too good to be true: Unrealistic offers or prizes
Phone Call Red Flags
  • Unsolicited calls: You didn't initiate contact
  • Pressure tactics: "You must act immediately!"
  • Requests for sensitive info: Asking for PINs, passwords, or personal details
  • Caller ID spoofing: Number appears legitimate but behavior is suspicious
  • Background noise: Unprofessional call center environment
How to Verify Legitimacy
For Emails and SMS
  1. Don't click any links in suspicious messages
  2. Go directly to the organization's official website
  3. Log into your account through the official site
  4. Check if there are any real alerts or messages
  5. Contact customer service using official phone numbers
For Phone Calls
  1. Hang up immediately if they ask for sensitive information
  2. Look up the organization's official phone number
  3. Call them back using the official number
  4. Ask to speak with the person who supposedly called you
  5. Verify if there are any real issues with your account
For Websites
  1. Check the URL carefully for misspellings
  2. Look for the padlock icon (HTTPS)
  3. Type the website address manually instead of clicking links
  4. Check the website's contact information
  5. Look for official certifications or trust seals
What to Do If You Fall for a Phishing Attack
Immediate Actions (First 30 minutes)
  • Change passwords: Immediately change passwords for affected accounts
  • Contact your bank: If financial information was compromised
  • Monitor accounts: Check all your accounts for unauthorized activity
  • Run antivirus: Scan your device for malware
Follow-up Actions (Next 24-48 hours)
  • Report the incident: Contact Africa Shield at *789*911#
  • File police report: If money was stolen
  • Alert contacts: Warn friends and family if your email was compromised
  • Monitor credit: Watch for unauthorized accounts or transactions
  • Update security: Enable two-factor authentication on all accounts
Prevention is Better Than Cure
  • Always verify before you trust
  • When in doubt, don't click or respond
  • Keep software and apps updated
  • Use reputable antivirus software
  • Educate family and friends about phishing
  • Report phishing attempts to help others

Network Security Basics

Intermediate 45 minutes

Understanding Network Security

Network security involves protecting your internet connection and the data that travels through it. Whether you're at home, work, or using public Wi-Fi, understanding network security helps protect your devices and information from cyber threats.

Types of Networks
Home Networks

Your personal Wi-Fi network at home, usually protected by a password and managed by your internet router.

Security Level: High (when properly configured)
Work Networks

Corporate networks with professional security measures, firewalls, and monitoring systems.

Security Level: Very High
Public Wi-Fi

Open networks in cafes, hotels, airports, and public spaces that anyone can access.

Security Level: Low Risk
Mobile Data

Internet connection through your mobile phone provider (3G, 4G, 5G).

Security Level: Medium-High
Common Network Threats
Man-in-the-Middle Attacks

Attackers intercept communication between your device and the internet, potentially stealing passwords, messages, and other sensitive data.

Example: Using fake Wi-Fi hotspots with names like "Free WiFi" to capture your internet traffic.
Packet Sniffing

Monitoring and capturing data packets traveling over a network to steal information.

Example: Criminals using special software to read unencrypted data on public Wi-Fi networks.
Evil Twin Attacks

Creating fake Wi-Fi networks that mimic legitimate ones to trick users into connecting.

Example: A fake "Starbucks_WiFi" network set up near a real Starbucks to steal customer data.
Denial of Service (DoS)

Overwhelming a network or website with traffic to make it unavailable to legitimate users.

Example: Flooding a company's website with fake requests to crash their online services.
Securing Your Home Network
Router Security
  • Change default passwords: Replace the default admin password with a strong, unique one
  • Update firmware: Keep your router's software updated
  • Use WPA3 encryption: Enable the strongest available Wi-Fi security
  • Hide network name: Don't broadcast your Wi-Fi network name (SSID)
  • Guest network: Set up a separate network for visitors
Wi-Fi Security
  • Strong Wi-Fi password: Use a complex password with at least 12 characters
  • Regular password changes: Change Wi-Fi password every 6 months
  • Monitor connected devices: Check which devices are connected to your network
  • Disable WPS: Turn off Wi-Fi Protected Setup to prevent easy hacking
  • MAC address filtering: Only allow specific devices to connect
Network Monitoring
  • Check connected devices: Regularly review what's connected to your network
  • Monitor data usage: Watch for unusual internet activity
  • Set up alerts: Get notifications for new device connections
  • Use network scanner apps: Tools to identify all devices on your network
  • Review router logs: Check for suspicious connection attempts
Public Wi-Fi Safety
Golden Rules for Public Wi-Fi
  1. Verify network names: Ask staff for the correct Wi-Fi name
  2. Avoid sensitive activities: No banking, shopping, or accessing personal accounts
  3. Use HTTPS websites: Look for the padlock icon in your browser
  4. Turn off auto-connect: Prevent automatic connections to open networks
  5. Use a VPN: Virtual Private Network encrypts your internet traffic
  6. Keep software updated: Ensure your device has latest security patches
  7. Log out completely: Sign out of all accounts when finished
What is a VPN?

A Virtual Private Network (VPN) creates a secure, encrypted tunnel between your device and the internet. Even if someone intercepts your data on public Wi-Fi, they can't read it because it's encrypted.

VPN Benefits:
  • Encrypts all your internet traffic
  • Hides your real IP address and location
  • Protects against man-in-the-middle attacks
  • Allows secure access to work networks
  • Bypasses geographic content restrictions
Recommended VPN Services:
  • ExpressVPN - Fast and reliable
  • NordVPN - Strong security features
  • Surfshark - Good value for money
  • ProtonVPN - Free tier available
Mobile Network Security
Securing Your Mobile Connection
  • Use mobile data when possible: Generally more secure than public Wi-Fi
  • Enable automatic updates: Keep your phone's operating system current
  • Use official app stores: Download apps only from Google Play or Apple App Store
  • Review app permissions: Don't give apps unnecessary access to your data
  • Enable remote wipe: Set up ability to erase your phone if stolen
  • Use screen locks: PIN, pattern, fingerprint, or face recognition
Mobile-Specific Threats
  • Malicious apps: Fake apps that steal data or send premium SMS
  • SIM swapping: Criminals transferring your number to their SIM card
  • Bluetooth attacks: Unauthorized access through Bluetooth connections
  • Location tracking: Apps collecting and sharing your location data
  • SMS interception: Intercepting text messages containing verification codes
Network Security Tools
Firewalls

Software or hardware that monitors and controls network traffic based on security rules.

Examples: Windows Defender Firewall, pfSense, Sophos XG Firewall
Antivirus Software

Programs that detect, prevent, and remove malicious software from your devices.

Examples: Bitdefender, Kaspersky, Norton, Avast
Network Scanners

Tools that help you see what devices are connected to your network.

Examples: Fing, Advanced IP Scanner, Angry IP Scanner
Network Monitoring

Software that tracks network performance and security in real-time.

Examples: PRTG, SolarWinds, Nagios
